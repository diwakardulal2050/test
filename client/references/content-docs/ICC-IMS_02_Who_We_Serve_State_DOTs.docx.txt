## Answer-first lead (first 120 words - the block AI engines will quote)
ICC-IMS manufactures inertial profilers, friction testers, and reference profilers for state departments of transportation, and provides network-level pavement data collection as a service.
Equipment is designed and built in the United States and certified to ASTM E950, AASHTO R56, AASHTO R57, and TxDOT specifications.
The IrisPRO Every Speed profiler is the only system that collects valid IRI at any vehicle speed - including stop-and-go and full-stop conditions - eliminating the approximately 12 mph minimum speed threshold that limits conventional inertial profilers.
ICC-IMS equipment has been in service at 39 state DOTs nationwide and 39 countries spanning 6 continents. Equipment can be procured directly or through the TXShare cooperative purchasing agreement.

## SECTION 2 - Equipment for state highway programs
### H2: Profilers
IrisPRO Every Speed (ESP) - the constraint everyone else still has Conventional inertial profilers stop producing valid data below roughly 12 mph. That makes urban corridors, work zones, congested interstates, and short segments like bridge decks either unmeasurable or unreliable. The ESP uses dual line lasers in each wheel path with a patented algorithm that holds accuracy through acceleration, deceleration, and full stops. Field testing shows negligible deviation in high-pass filtered profile and IRI between constant-speed, variable-speed, and full-stop runs. Certified to ASTM E950, AASHTO R56, AASHTO R57, and TxDOT specifications. US Patent 6,775,914. → IrisPRO Every Speed Spec sheet (hyperlink)
IrisPRO High Speed - network-level profiling for statewide programs ASTM E950 Class 1 inertial profiling built for the mileage volumes of a statewide collection cycle. → IrisPRO High Speed Spec sheet (hyperlink)
IrisPRO Lightweight - project-level and flexible deployment Compact profiling for project-level acceptance and assessments where a full survey vehicle is not warranted. → IrisPRO Lightweight Spec sheet (hyperlink)
IrisPRO Pave - profiling and 3D condition survey in a single pass LCMS-2 integration delivers 3D pavement scanning at speeds up to 75 mph with sub-millimeter crack detection, combined with ASTM Class 1 inertial profiling. One pass, one vehicle, both datasets. → IrisPRO Pave Spec sheet (hyperlink)
### H2: Friction testing
SFT Locked Wheel Friction Tester - ASTM E274 compliant skid trailer for highway safety friction programs and HFST acceptance testing. → SFT Locked Wheel Spec Sheet (hyperlink)
DFT Continuous Friction Tester - continuous friction measurement for network-level screening, identifying low-friction segments without spot-testing an entire route. → DFT Continuous Spec Sheet (hyperlink)
### H2: Reference devices - and why MAP-21 made this a compliance item
SurPRO Reference Profiler Under MAP-21 and the FAST Act, state DOTs are required to maintain certification sites for validating inertial profiler accuracy. That validation requires a walking-speed reference device. The SurPRO is that device.
This is not a discretionary purchase competing against other equipment for budget. It is the instrument that makes your entire profiling program defensible - including the data you report federally.
“The enhancements to the SurPro 5000 have made the device much easier to set up and use. The new user-friendly interface puts all the data right at your fingertips. The tablet displays all of the relevant data, in real time, as you are collecting. We have set up one of our local certification sites with the upgraded device and everything went much smoother.” John Senger, PE - Engineer of Pavement Technology, Illinois DOT

### H2: When it makes more sense to contract the collection than to run it
Not every program justifies owning and staffing a collection fleet. ICC-IMS runs statewide and corridor-level collection as a service, using the same equipment we manufacture - which means the team collecting your data built the sensor.

- HPMS data support - collection and formatting aligned to federal reporting requirements
- Corridor and project-level assessments - targeted collection for specific routes or construction acceptance
- 3D pavement condition surveys - LCMS-2 automated distress identification at highway speed
- Friction surveys - locked-wheel and continuous friction across the safety network
- HFST acceptance testing - verification that high friction surface treatments meet specification

## SECTION 4 - Data quality, documented
### H2: How we prove the data before you have to defend it
Data quality claims are easy to make. Here is the actual process:
- Equipment certification against the applicable ASTM and AASHTO standard
- Dedicated data validation sites used to verify system performance
- Routine verification procedures run on a defined interval, not on request
- Published pass/fail criteria - acceptance is a threshold, not a judgment call
- Documented corrective action process for data that does not pass
Automated QC runs through Unify Connect, which produces the quality documentation as an output of the workflow rather than as an after-the-fact report.

## SECTION 7 - Procurement
### H2: How to buy this through your contract vehicle
Option 1 - TXShare cooperative agreement ICC-IMS holds a Master Services Agreement for Pavement Analysis Services through TXShare, administered by the North Central Texas Council of Governments. TXShare is open to state agencies nationwide at no cost. Sign an Interlocal Agreement - typically executed within one business day - and contract without running a solicitation. Contracts are competitively sourced under Uniform Guidance (2 CFR 200).
Option 2 - Direct RFP or IFB For agencies required to solicit, ICC-IMS provides specification-grade documentation to support your procurement package: standards certifications, technical specifications, and vehicle compatibility data.
Option 3 - Sole source justification The Every Speed Profiler’s patented variable-speed capability (US Patent 6,775,914) is not available from another manufacturer. Where variable-speed IRI collection is a documented program requirement, that patent status is relevant to a sole-source justification.

## SECTION 9 - FAQ (FAQPage schema)
### H3: Does ICC-IMS equipment comply with ASTM E950?
Yes.
The IrisPRO Every Speed, IrisPRO High Speed, and IrisPRO Pave are ASTM E950 Class 1 certified inertial profilers. The IrisPRO Every Speed is additionally certified to AASHTO R56, AASHTO R57, and TxDOT specifications, and achieved 2026 iCART Certification in Illinois across all pavement types.
### H3: Can ICC-IMS profilers collect valid IRI at low speed?
Yes.
The IrisPRO Every Speed Profiler collects accurate, repeatable IRI at any vehicle speed, including accelerating, decelerating, and full-stop conditions. It uses dual line lasers in each wheel path with a patented algorithm (US Patent 6,775,914) that removes the approximately 12 mph minimum speed threshold applying to conventional inertial profilers. This makes it usable on urban networks, congested corridors, work zones, and bridge decks.
### H3: What reference device does ICC-IMS offer for DOT certification sites?
The SurPRO Reference Profiler is a walking-speed reference device used to validate inertial profiler accuracy at DOT certification sites, which states are required to maintain under MAP-21 and the FAST Act.
### H3: What data formats does ICC-IMS export for pavement management systems?
PPF, ERD, and PRO for ProVAL, plus XML, CSV, SHP, KML/KMZ, and Excel. Data is delivered into 15+ third-party pavement management platforms including Agile Assets, dTIMS, MicroPAVER, PAVER, StreetSaver, Cartegraph, Lucity, Brightly, and ArcGIS.
### H3: Does ICC-IMS provide data collection services, or only equipment?
Both, independently. State DOTs can purchase equipment to run in-house programs, contract ICC-IMS for statewide or corridor-level collection, or combine the two.
### H3: What cooperative purchasing contracts does ICC-IMS hold?
ICC-IMS holds a Master Services Agreement for Pavement Analysis Services through the TXShare Cooperative Purchasing Program, administered by the North Central Texas Council of Governments and open to public agencies nationwide at no cost.
### H3: Where is ICC-IMS equipment manufactured?
In Largo, Florida, at a 15,000 square foot facility, with $1.1M of parts held on hand for immediate service and support.
### H3: What is the repeatability of ICC-IMS profile data?
Cross-correlations typically range from 0.97 to 0.99 depending on surface type.
