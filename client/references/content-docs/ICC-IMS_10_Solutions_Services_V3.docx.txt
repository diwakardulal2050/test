### Body sections
#### What does a pavement condition survey produce?
A condition score for every segment. PCI or OCI, calculated from measured distress rather than visual estimation, so scores are comparable across the network and across years.
A mapped distress inventory. Every crack, patch, and rut recorded with its type, severity, extent, and location - the underlying evidence behind each score.
Pavement imagery. Right-of-way and pavement surface imagery for the network, reviewable without a site visit.
A QC record. Documentation of the validation the data passed, which matters when a consultant or a council member asks how the numbers were produced.
#### Why automated collection instead of a windshield survey?
Windshield surveys are fast and cheap and fail in three specific ways, all of which surface at the worst possible moment.
They are not repeatable - two raters disagree, and so does the same rater in a different season, which means an apparent year-over-year improvement may be rater drift. They are not defensible - “professional judgment” is a weak answer when a council member asks why one street outranked another with a paving contract on the agenda. And they do not scale - a network large enough to need prioritization is too large to rate consistently by hand.
Automated collection addresses all three. Same system, same method, every cycle, with every score traceable to recorded distress at a mapped location.
#### How is the survey conducted?
Collection uses IrisPRO Pave (hyperlink) vehicles with LCMS-2 3D scanning, at speeds up to 75 mph. Two consequences matter operationally.
No lane closures for most work. Collection happens in the normal traffic stream. For the agency this removes the traffic control plan that usually governs the schedule; for the public it means the survey is invisible.
3D rather than photographic detection. The LCMS-2 resolves surface geometry, so distress is identified by depth profile rather than by how it photographs. Detection does not degrade with lighting, shadow, or low surface contrast.
Data then passes through Unify Connect (hyperlink) for automated QC against acceptance criteria before delivery.
#### What happens to the data after the survey?
The survey is the input to a decision, not the end product. Most agencies proceed to a pavement management plan (hyperlink) that converts condition into a prioritized treatment program, and many continue to budget scenario analysis (/solutions/services/budget-scenario-analysis).
Data can be delivered into an existing system - StreetSaver, MicroPAVER, Cartegraph, Lucity, Brightly, dTIMS, Agile Assets, ArcGIS - or hosted in ESA (hyerlink). Agencies with no current system should not need to buy one to receive the data. See Integrations & Data Formats (hyperlink).
#### Who conducts the survey?
ICC-IMS crews, using equipment ICC-IMS manufactures. This is unusual: most survey providers buy their collection equipment from a third party, so a field problem becomes a support ticket to another company. Here it is an internal conversation, backed by $1.1M in on-hand parts inventory.

### Body sections
#### Why municipalities are inventorying sidewalks now
Two pressures converge.
Legal exposure is real and asymmetric. ADA obligations for pedestrian facilities in the public right-of-way are enforceable, and agencies without a documented transition plan and inventory are in a materially weaker position when a complaint arrives. The cost of an inventory is knowable in advance. The cost of a consent decree is not.
Sidewalks compete for pavement money without pavement data. Road condition is measured, scored, and defended with numbers. Sidewalks historically are not, which means sidewalk needs lose budget arguments to road needs by default rather than on merit.
An inventory addresses both: it produces the documentation the legal posture requires and the numbers the budget argument requires.
Note: this page describes accessibility assessment services and should not be read as legal advice on ADA compliance obligations. Agencies should confirm their specific obligations with counsel. Recommend a standing disclaimer line in the page footer.
#### What the survey measures
Sidewalk assessment differs from pavement assessment in that condition is only half the question. A sidewalk in excellent structural condition can be entirely non-compliant if its cross slope exceeds the limit.
The survey therefore captures both: condition (surface distress, vertical displacement, deterioration) and compliance geometry (cross slope, running slope, width, obstructions, curb ramp configuration and detectable warning presence).

### Body sections
#### What a pavement management plan actually answers
Condition data tells an agency what shape the network is in. It does not say what to do. The plan answers four questions the data alone cannot:
What treatment does each segment need? A road at PCI 70 and a road at PCI 40 need different work - and the road at 70 is frequently the better investment, because preserving a good road is cheaper than rebuilding a failed one.
In what order? Ranking by worst-first is intuitive, politically comfortable, and usually the most expensive strategy available. A defensible plan optimizes across the network instead.
In what year? A multi-year program that fits actual funding, not a wish list.
At what cost, with what result? Projected network condition under the recommended program, so the plan can be measured against outcomes later.
#### Why worst-first costs more
This is the single most useful thing a pavement management plan tells an elected official, and it is counterintuitive enough that it should be stated explicitly on the page.
Pavement deterioration is not linear. A road holds a serviceable condition for years, then declines rapidly. Treatment cost follows the same curve in reverse: a preservation treatment applied while a road is still in fair condition costs a fraction of the reconstruction that becomes necessary once it fails.
Spending the whole budget on the worst roads means every remaining road continues sliding toward the expensive end of that curve. The result is a network that gets worse every year while the agency spends more each year, which is exactly the trap most agencies describe when they first call.

#### How the plan is built
- Network segmentation and inventory - establishing the units the plan operates on.
- Condition assessment - from a condition survey (hyperlink) or existing agency data.
- Deterioration modeling - projecting condition forward by segment and pavement type.
- Treatment matching - mapping condition and distress type to appropriate treatments at agency unit costs.
- Optimization and prioritization - producing the program that delivers the best network outcome for the available money.
- Program development - a multi-year plan with annual project lists and cost estimates.
Agencies with current condition data can start at step 3.

### Body sections
#### Answering everyone’s question
Scenario analysis changes the question from “should we spend more on roads” to “which of these four outcomes do we want.” Those are different conversations, and the second one is winnable because it puts the consequence of each choice on the record before the choice is made rather than five years afterward.
#### What a scenario set looks like
Scenario
Question it answers
Status quo
If funding stays flat, what condition is the network in five years from now?
Reduced funding
What does a cut actually cost, in condition and in future dollars?
Condition hold
What annual funding is required to keep the network where it is today?
Target condition
What would it take to reach your stated network condition goal, and by when?
Backlog elimination
What would it cost to clear deferred maintenance, and over what period?
Each scenario projects network condition, backlog, and cost year by year over the horizon.
#### Why the “condition hold” number matters most
The single most useful figure produced is the annual funding required simply to keep the network from getting worse.
It reframes the entire discussion. A budget below that number is not “level funding” - it is a decision to let the network decline, with the deferred cost accruing to a future budget. Putting that number in front of a council converts an abstract funding debate into a specific, dated choice with a price attached.
Agencies frequently find that the condition-hold number is lower than expected when preservation treatments are applied early, which is the encouraging half of the same finding.

Locked-wheel testing produces a Friction Number at discrete locations and is the reference method for compliance and acceptance in most state specifications. #### HFST acceptance testing
High Friction Surface Treatment is installed at specific locations - curves, ramps, and approaches with documented wet-weather crash history - precisely because friction there is safety-critical. Acceptance testing verifies the installed treatment actually delivers the specified friction.
This is not a procedural formality. HFST is expensive per lane-mile and is justified by a safety benefit that only materializes if the installation performs. Acceptance testing is the verification that the safety investment worked, and it is also the agency’s documentation that it did.

#### The problem with the one-time survey
An agency commissions a survey, receives a report and a plan, and executes against it. #### What an ongoing program provides
Current data, always. Condition data refreshed on a defined cycle instead of decaying between one-off engagements.
Trends that mean something. Year-over-year comparison is only valid when measurement is consistent. Same provider, same equipment, same method, same processing produces trend data an agency can act on and defend. Data assembled from three different vendors over eight years does not.

#### Why continuity of provider matters more here than elsewhere
Procurement practice generally favors re-competing contracts, and for many services that is exactly right. Condition monitoring is a case where the calculus is different, and it is worth saying so plainly rather than hoping the client works it out.
Condition scores are produced by a measurement chain - equipment, calibration, processing, and rating methodology. Change any link and the scores shift. A network that appears to have declined between 2024 and 2027 may simply have been measured differently. Agencies that switch providers each cycle accumulate a series of snapshots rather than a trend, and the trend is where the value is.

### FAQ block
How often should we re-survey our road network? Most agencies survey on a two- or three-year cycle. Annual surveys suit large networks, rapidly deteriorating networks, and agencies executing an aggressive treatment program that needs close tracking.
What is included in an ongoing monitoring program? Scheduled re-survey, updated condition data, a refreshed management plan, year-over-year trend analysis, and continuous dashboard access between surveys.
Why does using the same provider matter for trend data? Condition scores depend on the equipment, calibration, processing, and rating methodology used to produce them. Changing providers can shift scores independently of any actual change in the pavement, which breaks year-over-year comparability.
Can we show our council that conditions improved? Yes. Trend data on a consistent measurement basis is what demonstrates whether a funding increase produced a measurable result, which is generally what determines whether it continues.
Do we keep access to our data between surveys? Yes, through ESA and Unify Inform dashboards.
How long have your longest clients been with you? Lake County, Illinois has contracted annually with IMS since 1986. The City of Dunwoody, Georgia has completed three surveys over twelve years.
