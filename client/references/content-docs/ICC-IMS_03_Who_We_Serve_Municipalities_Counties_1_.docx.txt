## Answer-first lead (first 120 words)
ICC-IMS provides pavement condition surveys, pavement management plans, and budget scenario analysis for cities, counties, and regional agencies.
A typical engagement begins with an automated survey of the entire road network, producing GIS-mapped PCI or OCI condition data for every segment. That data becomes a prioritized management plan showing:

## SECTION 1 - The actual problem
### H2: Your problem usually isn’t technical. It’s political.
You already know your roads are deteriorating. What you need is a defensible answer to four questions someone is about to ask you in a public meeting:
- “Which streets are you fixing, and why those?”
- “What happens if we fund you at last year’s level instead?”
- “Why does this road need work when the one by my house is worse?”
- “How do we know this is a good use of the money?”
Answering those without data is a losing position.
Answering them with a network-wide condition survey, a prioritized plan, and a funding scenario model is a very strong one.
That is what this engagement produces.

### H2: The Process that Produces Actionable Results for your Municipality
Step 1 - Network condition survey Our vehicles drive every segment of your network. Automated 3D imaging and laser measurement capture distress data - cracking, rutting, raveling, patching - without lane closures or crews in traffic. You do not need to provide staff. Typical duration: days to a few weeks depending on network size.
Step 2 - Condition data, mapped Distress data is processed into PCI or OCI scores for every segment and delivered on a GIS map. You can see your whole network by condition, color-coded, at a glance - and so can anyone you show it to. Deliverable: GIS-mapped condition inventory, HD imagery of every segment.
Step 3 - Pavement management plan Our engineers apply treatment logic and prioritization to the condition data: which segments get crack seal, which get slurry seal, which need overlay, which need reconstruction - and critically, the order that produces the most network condition per dollar. Deliverable: prioritized multi-year treatment plan with cost estimates and remaining service life projections.
Step 4 - Budget scenario analysis We model your network under different funding levels. What does the network look like in five years at current funding? At a 20% increase? At a 20% cut? What does deferring maintenance actually cost? Deliverable: side-by-side funding scenarios with projected network condition and backlog.
Step 5 - Council presentation We build and, if you want, deliver the presentation to your council or board in simple, easy-to-understand language & graphics for non-engineers. Technical pavement data translated for people who do not work in pavement - maps, before-and-after imagery, plain-language funding options. This is the step most vendors do not offer but it is frequently the step that determines whether the plan gets funded.
Step 6 - Ongoing monitoring Condition data ages. Annual or biennial re-surveys, delivered through a web dashboard, let you track whether the program is working and show year-over-year progress - which is what turns a one-time study into a sustained funding stream. Deliverable: SaaS dashboard access, trend reporting, updated priorities each cycle.

## SECTION 4 - The budget argument
### H2: What deferred maintenance actually costs with real data to back it up
Pavement deterioration is not linear. A road in good condition can be kept in good condition cheaply. Once it fails, the cost jumps by roughly an order of magnitude.
- Preventive treatment on a road in good condition: approximately $1 per square yard
- Reconstruction of a failed road: approximately $6–10 per square yard
- Agencies running data-driven pavement management extend average pavement life by 5–7 years (reference: FHWA)
The implication for a council presentation is straightforward: every year a street in fair condition is deferred, it moves closer to a treatment category that costs six to ten times more. A management plan is not a study. It is the difference between spending $1 now and $8 later.
GASB 34 reporting. Agencies using the modified approach need documented condition assessments performed at least every three years, with results showing assets are being preserved at or above an established condition level. A network condition survey and management plan produce exactly that documentation.
ADA and PROWAG exposure. Sidewalk non-compliance is a litigation risk with a well-established plaintiff bar. An inventoried, prioritized, actively-worked transition plan is materially better legal ground than no inventory at all.

## SECTION 9 - FAQ (FAQPage schema)
### H3: How long does a pavement condition survey take?
Field collection typically takes days to a few weeks depending on network size, and does not require lane closures or staff support from your agency. Processing, analysis, and plan development follow. A complete engagement from survey through delivered management plan generally runs several weeks to a few months depending on scope.
### H3: What is PCI and how is it calculated?
PCI (Pavement Condition Index) is a 0–100 score representing pavement surface condition. It is calculated from the type, severity, and extent of observed distresses - cracking, rutting, raveling, patching, and others - measured across a pavement segment. ICC-IMS collects distress data using automated 3D imaging and laser measurement, which removes the rater-to-rater variability of manual windshield surveys.
### H3: Do we need to buy software to work with ICC-IMS?
No. ICC-IMS delivers data into 15+ third-party pavement management platforms including StreetSaver, MicroPAVER, PAVER, Cartegraph, Lucity, Brightly, dTIMS, Agile Assets, and ArcGIS. Software is available if you want it, but it is never a condition of service.
### H3: Can ICC-IMS help present findings to our city council?
Yes. Presentations to elected officials is a defined service. ICC-IMS builds council-ready presentations that translate technical pavement data for non-technical decision-makers, and can deliver the presentation directly.
### H3: Does a pavement condition survey satisfy GASB 34 requirements?
A network condition assessment and management plan provide the documented condition data GASB 34’s modified approach requires - condition assessments at least every three years, with results demonstrating assets are preserved at or above an established condition level. Confirm specific reporting requirements with your finance department and auditor.
### H3: How do we procure ICC-IMS services without going through an RFP?
Through the TXShare cooperative purchasing program. TXShare is open to public agencies nationwide at no cost. Sign an Interlocal Agreement - typically executed within one business day - and contract directly with no solicitation required.
### H3: Do you survey sidewalks as well as streets?
Yes. Sidewalk condition and ADA/PROWAG compliance surveys are a separate service, often run alongside a pavement survey. Deliverables include inventoried non-compliance, prioritization for remediation, and documentation supporting an ADA transition plan.
### H3: How often should a city re-survey its network?
Most agencies re-survey on a one to three year cycle. Annual re-survey suits larger or faster-deteriorating networks and agencies reporting progress to a council each budget cycle. Three years is generally the outer limit before data is too stale to defend a programming decision - and is also the GASB 34 modified approach interval.
### H3: What does a pavement management program cost?
Cost depends on network size, survey type, and deliverables. The more useful framing for a budget conversation is the comparison: preventive treatment runs roughly $1 per square yard while reconstruction runs roughly $6–10. A management plan’s value is in redirecting spending toward the cheaper category. Contact us for scoping against your specific network.
