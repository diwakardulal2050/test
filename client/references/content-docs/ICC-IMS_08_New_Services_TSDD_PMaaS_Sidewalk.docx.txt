### H1: Next Generation Pavement Structural Assessment Without Closures
## Answer-first lead
A Traffic Speed Deflection Device (TSDD) measures pavement structural condition while traveling at highway speed, eliminating the lane closures, work zones, and crew exposure required by conventional Falling Weight Deflectometer testing.
This makes structural evaluation practical at the network level rather than only at the project level.
ICC-IMS provides TSDD-based network structural evaluation and has published peer-reviewed research on the methodology in the Transportation Research Record (TRR), demonstrating a platform that determines effective structural number (SNeff) without requiring pavement layer thickness data - the input agencies most often lack at network scale.
ICC-IMS also operates FastFWD and Ground Penetrating Radar for project-level structural assessment.

## SECTION 1 - The problem
### H2: Every FWD test on a live highway is a lane closure
Falling Weight Deflectometer testing is the established method for measuring pavement structural condition, and it works. But it is a stationary test. Every test point on a live highway means a lane closure, a work zone, traffic control cost, and crews standing next to moving traffic. Plus, it’s just that, one point at a time.
That constraint has a consequence most agencies have simply accepted: structural testing gets reserved for project-level work. Network-level decisions get made on surface condition alone.
## SECTION 2 - Why surface condition alone produces wrong answers
### H2: PCI does not tell you what is happening underneath – surface condition is not enough
The substructure of a road - which you cannot see - is the single largest determinant of long-term pavement performance. Surface condition indices like PCI describe the top. They are frequently independent of what is happening below it.
That gap produces two expensive errors, in opposite directions:
Error 1 - Money spent on pavement that is going to fail anyway. A preservation treatment or thin overlay applied to a structurally inadequate road is a potential total loss. The surface looks treated. The road fails early anyway.
Error 2 - Money spent on treatment heavier than necessary. Alligator cracking is commonly read as structural failure. But age-related alligator cracking from an oxidized, brittle binder can occur on a structurally sound pavement that needs only resurfacing. Without structural data, that road gets a rehabilitation it did not need.
Research has repeatedly found that network-level decisions made on surface condition alone differ significantly from those made with deflection data included. Where agencies use surface treatments - microsurfacing, seal coating, slurry seal - visual evaluation is further compromised, because those treatments conceal the very distresses the evaluation depends on.
The practical implication: structural data does not just refine treatment selection, it expands the decision matrix. Without it you are working from one column of the treatment table. With it, you have access to the whole table - including the case where surface condition suggests preventive maintenance but structural condition says do nothing, because PM on a failing structure is wasted money.
## SECTION 3 - What TSDD changes
### H2: Structural data for the whole network, not a sample
TSDD removes the constraint that made network-level structural testing impractical:
- No lane closures. Collection happens in the traffic stream at highway speed.
- No work zones and no crew exposure. The safety cost of structural testing effectively goes to zero.
- No traffic control budget. Which for many agencies was a larger line item than the testing itself.
- Network coverage instead of sample points. Continuous measurement across the route rather than discrete tests spaced hundreds of feet apart.
### H3: The layer thickness problem, and how our methodology handles it
Traditional backcalculation requires knowing the thickness of each pavement layer. At project level, cores can be taken. At network level, that information is rarely available - most agencies do not have reliable layer thickness records across their full system, which has been a fundamental barrier to using deflection data for network decisions.
ICC-IMS engineers published a platform that determines effective structural number (SNeff) using inputs readily available from the testing itself, without requiring layer thickness data. The work was published in the Transportation Research Record (Nakhaei & Poorbaugh, Vol. 2679, Issue 10, 2025) with a Mississippi Department of Transportation case study.

### H3: Example
A one-inch mill and overlay is programmed based on surface condition. Structural data shows subsurface problems requiring a deeper mill - the programmed treatment would have failed prematurely, wasting the entire expenditure.
The reverse case is just as common and just as valuable. A four-inch mill and overlay is programmed. Structural testing shows subgrade support is sound and a two-inch mill will perform. The savings on a single corridor can exceed the cost of the structural survey.

## SECTION 5 - Complementary structural services
### H2: The full structural toolkit
FastFWD - Fast-Falling Weight Deflectometer for project-level structural evaluation. Applies a known dynamic load simulating traffic loading and measures deflection response to determine load-carrying capacity and remaining service life. Meets AASHTO R32 specifications.
Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) - Collected per ASTM D4748 and frequently paired with deflection data to determine remaining pavement life, structural number, pavement modulus, and subgrade modulus. GPR also supports bridge deck assessment, concrete evaluation, and utility locating and mapping.
Traffic data integration - FWD and TSDD interpretation improves substantially with reliable traffic data, particularly heavy vehicle composition and AADT. We incorporate it where available.

## SECTION 7 - FAQ (FAQPage schema)
### H3: What is a Traffic Speed Deflection Device (TSDD)?
A TSDD measures pavement structural response to loading while traveling at highway speed, rather than from a stationary position. This allows continuous structural data collection across a road network without lane closures, work zones, or traffic control.
### H3: How is TSDD different from a Falling Weight Deflectometer?
An FWD is stationary. It applies an impact load at discrete test points, which on a live roadway requires a lane closure and work zone at each location. A TSDD collects continuously at traffic speed. FWD remains the standard for detailed project-level evaluation; TSDD makes structural evaluation practical at network scale.
### H3: Does TSDD testing require lane closures?
No. Collection occurs at highway speed within the traffic stream, which eliminates lane closures, work zones, traffic control cost, and crew exposure to traffic.
### H3: Do you need pavement layer thickness data for network-level structural evaluation?
Not with our methodology. ICC-IMS engineers published a platform in the Transportation Research Record that determines effective structural number (SNeff) from inputs available in the testing itself, without requiring layer thickness - which is rarely available to agencies at network scale.
### H3: Why isn’t PCI enough for treatment decisions?
PCI describes surface condition, which can be independent of structural condition. A structurally inadequate road can present an acceptable PCI, and a structurally sound road can show alligator cracking caused by binder oxidation rather than structural failure. Treating on surface condition alone produces both premature failures and unnecessarily heavy treatments.
### H3: What is Structural Index (SI)?
A 0–100 score expressing pavement structural adequacy, integrated into the Overall Condition Index alongside surface condition. Above 75 indicates structural adequacy suitable for light surface treatments; 50–75 typically requires additional thickness; below 50 generally indicates a need for reconstruction.
### H3: Does ICC-IMS also offer FWD testing?
Yes. ICC-IMS operates FastFWD equipment meeting AASHTO R32 specifications for project-level structural evaluation, and Ground Penetrating Radar collected per ASTM D4748.
### H3: Has ICC-IMS published research on TSDD methodology?
Yes. ICC-IMS engineers published network-level structural evaluation methodology using TSDD data in the Transportation Research Record, Vol. 2679 Issue 10 (2025), including a Mississippi Department of Transportation case study.

## SECTION 1 - The problem this solves
### H2: You paid for a pavement management plan. What now?
Most agencies have commissioned a pavement management study at some point. A consultant surveyed the network, produced a report, presented it, and left.
Then reality arrived. A water main project changed the paving schedule. A funding source appeared and had to be spent. Two streets failed early. Council asked for a corridor that was not in the plan.

### H1: Real data, not subjectivity
## Answer-first lead
ICC-IMS performs sidewalk surface testing using the Sidewalk Surface Tester (SST), which measures sidewalk surface condition and smoothness objectively and repeatably rather than through visual inspection.
The resulting data supports ADA and PROWAG compliance assessment, trip hazard identification and prioritization, construction acceptance for new sidewalk installation, and liability documentation for municipal risk management.
Because measurement is instrument-based, results are consistent between operators and comparable year over year - which is what makes a sidewalk program defensible if compliance or liability is ever challenged.
## SECTION 1 - Why measurement beats inspection
### H2: “Someone walked it and made notes” is a weak position
Most sidewalk assessment is still done by walking the network with a clipboard or tablet. That produces three problems:
Inconsistency. Two inspectors rate the same segment differently. The same inspector rates differently in hour six than hour one.
Non-comparability. If this year’s assessment used different people than last year’s, the change between them is not measurable.
Weak defensibility. In an ADA complaint or a trip-and-fall claim, “our inspector judged it acceptable” is a materially weaker position than a measured value against a documented threshold.
Instrument-based measurement removes all three.
## SECTION 2 - What we measure and what it is used for
### H2: Applications
ADA and PROWAG compliance - Surface conditions bearing on accessibility, supporting transition plan development and prioritization.
Trip hazard identification - Vertical displacements located, measured, and prioritized by severity rather than by whoever reported them.
Construction acceptance - Objective verification that newly constructed sidewalk meets specified surface criteria before acceptance, rather than after the first complaint.
Liability documentation - A measured, dated, defensible record of network condition and remediation activity.
Program prioritization - Where the remediation budget produces the most accessibility improvement.
## SECTION 3 - How it fits the sidewalk program
### H2: Surface testing plus compliance inventory
Sidewalk surface testing is normally run alongside ADA/PROWAG compliance inventory, which captures missing curb ramps, non-compliant cross-slopes and running slopes, obstructions, and detectable warning surfaces. Together they produce a complete picture:
- What is non-compliant?
- What is hazardous?
- What should we fix first?
Results are managed in ESWA, ICC-IMS’s sidewalk management platform, which maintains the inventory, tracks compliance status, and documents remediation over time.

## SECTION 4 - FAQ
### H3: What is sidewalk surface testing?
Instrument-based measurement of sidewalk surface condition and smoothness, producing objective and repeatable values rather than visual inspection ratings.
### H3: How is this different from an ADA compliance survey?
An ADA/PROWAG compliance survey inventories accessibility features - curb ramps, slopes, obstructions, detectable warnings - against regulatory requirements. Surface testing measures the condition and smoothness of the walking surface itself. Most agencies benefit from both, collected together.
### H3: Can sidewalk surface data be used for construction acceptance?
Yes. Objective surface measurement allows verification against specified criteria at acceptance, before the agency assumes maintenance responsibility.
### H3: How does this reduce liability exposure?
It replaces subjective inspection with a measured, dated record of condition and remediation. A documented, prioritized, actively-worked program is a materially stronger position in an ADA complaint or trip-and-fall claim than an undocumented one.
### H3: Where is sidewalk data stored and managed?
In ESWA, ICC-IMS’s sidewalk management platform, which maintains inventory, tracks compliance status, and documents remediation over time.
