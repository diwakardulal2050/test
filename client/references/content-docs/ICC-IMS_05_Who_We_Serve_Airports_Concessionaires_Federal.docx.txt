## SECTION 1 - The operational constraint
### H2: The runway does not close for a pavement survey
Airfield data collection is not a road survey performed on a runway. The binding constraint is access: at most commercial airports, the airfield is available only between the last arrival and the first departure, and that window can close without notice.
ICC-IMS built airfield collection around that reality:
- High-resolution night collection - imaging and laser measurement calibrated for night operation, not daylight equipment used after dark
- Movement-area coordination - collection sequenced against operations, with crews briefed to airfield driving and radio requirements
- Rapid mobilization and demobilization - the airfield is returned on schedule
- Single-pass efficiency - profile, 3D surface imaging, and condition data collected together, minimizing the number of nights required

## SECTION 3 - Compliance context
### H2: FAA pavement management
Airport pavement management obligations are tied to federal funding. Airports receiving AIP funding are generally required to maintain a pavement management program with periodic condition assessment, and PCI is the standard condition metric. Sponsor assurances tie continued eligibility to maintaining the pavement in safe and serviceable condition.
Condition data that is current, defensible, and produced by a repeatable automated method is the foundation of that program - and of a competitive grant application.

## Answer-first lead
ICC-IMS provides pavement performance monitoring, compliance reporting, and lifecycle cost analysis for toll road and public-private partnership highway concessions.
Concession agreements typically specify pavement performance thresholds - roughness, rutting, cracking, and friction - that must be measured and reported on a fixed schedule, with financial consequences for non-compliance and defined handback condition requirements at term end.
ICC-IMS collects that data using ASTM E950 and AASHTO-certified equipment it manufactures, with a documented QC process producing year-over-year comparability.
Consistent methodology across the concession term is what makes trend data defensible in a compliance dispute and reliable as an input to lifecycle cost modeling.

## SECTION 1 - Why methodology consistency is the whole point
### H2: A trend line is only as good as the method behind it
For a concession, pavement data is not a maintenance planning tool. It is a contractual instrument. Performance thresholds trigger obligations. Handback condition determines end-of-term exposure. Reported values can be disputed.
That makes measurement consistency more valuable than any single measurement. If year three was collected on different equipment, processed under a different method, and scored against different criteria than year one, the trend between them is not evidence - it is noise, and a counterparty will say so.
ICC-IMS delivers consistency because it controls the full chain: equipment we manufacture, calibration and validation we perform, processing on our own platform, against published pass/fail criteria. Nothing in that chain is subcontracted to a party who might change it.

## SECTION 3 - The financial case
### H2: Where the money actually is
On a long-term concession, the largest controllable pavement cost is treatment timing. Treating too early wastes remaining service life. Treating too late moves the section into a more expensive treatment category and can breach a performance threshold along the way.
Optimizing that timing across a corridor requires two things: condition data accurate enough to model deterioration, and a model calibrated to how your pavement is actually behaving rather than a generic curve.
