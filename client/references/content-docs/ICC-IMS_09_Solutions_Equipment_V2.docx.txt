### Body sections
#### What makes the Every Speed different from a conventional inertial profiler?
Conventional inertial profilers derive elevation by combining an accelerometer reference with a height sensor. Below roughly 12 mph, the accelerometer signal degrades relative to noise and the derived profile is no longer valid. Every conventional profiler carries this constraint, which is why operators are trained to abort and re-run a section when traffic forces them below threshold.
With the ESP, there is no minimum mph requirement.
The ESP resolves this with dual line lasers in each wheel path and a proprietary algorithm that maintains a valid profile independent of vehicle speed. The result is data that stays valid through signalized intersections, congestion, work zone queues, and complete stops.
The practical difference is not incremental.
On an urban network, a conventional profiler produces a collection day full of voided sections and re-runs. The ESP produces a complete network.
#### Where does variable-speed profiling actually matter?
- Urban and suburban arterials with signalized intersections and frequent stops
- Work zones where lane closures and flagging operations force variable speed
- Congested corridors where maintaining a constant collection speed is not possible
- Interchange ramps, toll plazas, and approach lanes where speed change is inherent to the geometry
- Airport service roads and constrained facilities where operating speed is restricted
- Acceptance testing on short project segments where there is insufficient runway to reach and hold profiling speed

#### What is the IrisPRO High Speed built for?
Statewide network collection is a volume problem before it is a precision problem. An agency profiling its full network annually is running the same system for months across every surface type, contractor, and construction era in the state. What determines whether that program succeeds is consistency: the same system producing comparable numbers in June that it produced in March, so that year-over-year trends reflect pavement condition rather than equipment drift.
The HSP is configured for that use case - sustained highway-speed collection, ASTM E950 Class 1 accuracy, and the mechanical simplicity that keeps a production vehicle in service.
#### How does the High Speed compare to the Every Speed?
Both are ASTM-certified inertial profilers built by ICC-IMS on the same MARS mounting system. The difference is the collection environment they are optimized for.

### Body sections
#### What does a locked-wheel friction tester measure?
ASTM E274 testing works by bringing a test wheel to a full lock at a controlled speed on a wetted pavement surface and measuring the resulting friction force. The result is reported as a Friction Number - the friction force expressed as a percentage of the vertical load, at a stated test speed.
Two properties of the method matter for programs built on it:
The test tire determines what is measured. A ribbed tire (ASTM E501) is primarily sensitive to pavement microtexture and is relatively insensitive to water depth. A smooth tire (ASTM E524) is sensitive to macrotexture and to drainage, which makes it the better indicator of wet-weather crash risk at speed. Many state programs run both. Friction Numbers from different tires are not interchangeable, which is why FN values are always reported with tire type and test speed.
Locked-wheel is a spot measurement, not a continuous one. Each test produces a value for a discrete location. This makes locked-wheel testing the standard for compliance, acceptance, and investigation, and makes continuous friction measurement the better tool for screening a full network.
See the DFT Continuous Friction Tester  /solutions/equipment/dft-continuous  (hyperlink) for the continuous approach - most mature state programs use both.
#### What do agencies use the SFT for?
Highway safety friction programs. Routine friction testing on a defined cycle, with results feeding a safety management process that flags locations falling below an agency threshold.
HFST acceptance testing. High Friction Surface Treatment is installed specifically to raise friction at curves, ramps, and approaches with a documented wet-weather crash history. Acceptance testing verifies that the installed treatment delivers the specified friction. Because HFST is applied precisely where friction is safety-critical, acceptance testing is not a formality - it is the verification that the safety investment worked.
Crash-site investigation. Friction measurement at locations with a crash pattern, supporting engineering analysis and, frequently, tort liability defense.
Network friction inventory. Systematic friction data across a network to identify deficient sections before a crash pattern develops.

### Body sections
#### What is continuous friction measurement?
A locked-wheel test answers “what is the friction at this point?” Continuous friction measurement answers “how does friction vary along this entire route?”
The difference matters because friction is not uniform. A section that passes a spot test at station 12+00 may have a polished, low-friction stretch two hundred feet later - a stretch a discrete test will miss entirely unless it happens to land on it. Continuous measurement removes the sampling problem: every foot of the route is measured.
The trade-off is that locked-wheel testing remains the reference method for compliance and acceptance in most state specifications. In practice, mature programs use continuous measurement to screen the network and identify candidate sections, then apply locked-wheel testing to the sections that screening flags. Screening finds the problem; the reference method documents it.
#### Network-level friction screening
For a state DOT, or a large agency, the operational value is coverage. Discrete testing across a full network is a sampling exercise constrained by how many tests a crew can complete in a season. Continuous measurement converts friction from a sampled attribute into a mapped, wall-to-wall dataset.
That dataset supports a friction management program in the sense the FHWA advocates: systematic identification of friction-deficient locations before a crash pattern develops, rather than investigation after one has.
#### Runway and airfield friction testing
Runway friction is an operational and regulatory requirement, not a maintenance preference. Airport operators conduct friction surveys on a defined schedule, and results drive maintenance actions including rubber removal and surface treatment. Continuous measurement along the full runway length is the expected data form - friction values at sampled points do not satisfy the requirement, because the friction condition that matters is the one in the touchdown zone where rubber accumulates.

### SurPRO Reference Profiler: The Reference Standard for DOT Certification Sites
### Answer-first lead
The SurPRO is a walking-speed reference profiler manufactured by ICC-IMS in Largo, Florida. It establishes the reference longitudinal profile against which inertial profiling systems are certified and validated.
The SurPRO uses patented technology to obtain reference profile data with 99.9% repeatability and accuracy. It is the only reference device to meet FHWA’s Golden Reference Profiling Device requirements.
Under MAP-21 and the FAST Act, state DOTs are required to report pavement ride quality data collected by certified equipment, and certification requires a reference profile from a device of documented accuracy.
The SurPRO is the device that produces it.
Illinois DOT uses the SurPRO 5000 in its pavement technology program.
The SurPRO walking profiler is the most accurate device for measuring surface profile and roughness characteristics of any placed surface, including roads, runways, and other structures. It is a Class 1 profiling unit and uses patented technology.
The SurPRO is a fully automated, portable unit. It weighs just 42 pounds. It can be brought onto any job site and be collecting data in a matter of minutes. The SurPRO collects true elevation profiles, displays the results, saves the data, and executes user-configurable analysis to provide numerical and graphical representation of the profile and roughness indices.

### Body sections
#### Why a state DOT needs a reference profiler
The chain of obligation runs as follows:
- Under MAP-21 and the FAST Act, state DOTs report pavement condition data, including ride quality, to FHWA.
- That data must come from certified inertial profiling equipment.
- Certification under AASHTO R56 requires the profiler to be run against a certification site - a prepared pavement section with a known, accurate reference profile.
- The reference profile must be established by a device more accurate than the equipment being certified.
A reference profiler is therefore not an optional accuracy accessory. It is the instrument at the base of the measurement chain, and without it a state cannot certify its own equipment or its contractors’.
#### What a reference profiler is used for
Establishing and re-establishing certification sites. A certification site’s reference profile is not permanent - the pavement changes, and the site must be re-profiled on a schedule.
Certifying agency and contractor equipment. States that require contractor profilers to be certified before acceptance testing need reference data to certify against.
Investigating disputes. When a contractor’s profiler and an agency’s profiler disagree on a smoothness incentive payment, the reference device settles it. On a large paving contract with ride incentives and disincentives, a single disputed section can exceed the cost of the reference profiler.
Validating a new or repaired system. After sensor replacement, recalibration, or a software change, a reference profile confirms the system still produces valid data.
### Applications
- Manage and maintain any placed surface
- Compare pavement profile with specifications for contract bonus/penalty determination
- Identify and correct surface defects to achieve target roughness indices (using bump/dip analysis)
- Establish calibration site benchmarks for contractor and vendor equipment validation
- Meet Data Quality Management Plan (DQMP) requirements
- Test bridges before approaches are in place
- Sidewalk smoothness
- Testing road cross slopes
- Testing drainage slopes
#### How does a walking-speed reference profiler achieve higher accuracy?
An inertial profiler infers elevation by combining accelerometer and height sensor data while moving at speed, which introduces the dependencies that make certification necessary in the first place - speed, vehicle dynamics, and sensor response.
A walking-speed reference device removes those dependencies. Operating at walking pace, it measures the surface directly and continuously without relying on an inertial reference to separate pavement profile from vehicle motion. The trade-off is production rate: a reference device profiles a certification site, not a network. That is precisely its role.

### FAQ block
What is a reference profiler? A reference profiler is a high-accuracy, walking-speed device used to establish the reference longitudinal profile of a pavement section. That reference profile is what inertial profiling systems are certified and validated against.
Why does a DOT need a reference profiler? Under MAP-21 and the FAST Act, state DOTs report pavement ride quality data collected with certified equipment. Certification under AASHTO R56 requires running the profiler against a certification site with a known reference profile, and that reference profile must be established by a device of documented higher accuracy.
What is a pavement certification site? A certification site is a prepared pavement section with an accurately established reference profile, used to verify that an inertial profiling system produces valid results before it is used for data collection or acceptance testing.
Can the SurPRO be used to settle a smoothness dispute? Yes. When an agency profiler and a contractor profiler disagree on a section subject to ride incentives, a reference profile from a device of documented accuracy is the basis for resolution.
How fast does the SurPRO operate? The SurPRO operates at walking speed, which is what allows it to achieve reference-grade accuracy. It is designed to profile certification sites and test sections, not networks.
Which agencies use the SurPRO? Illinois DOT uses the SurPRO 5000 in its pavement technology program.
What is the difference between the SurPRO and an inertial profiler? An inertial profiler collects at highway speed and derives profile from combined accelerometer and height sensor data. The SurPRO operates at walking speed and measures the surface directly, achieving the higher accuracy required to certify inertial equipment. The two are complementary: the SurPRO certifies the equipment that collects the network.

Make them findable. PDFs index in search and are cited by AI engines. Use descriptive filenames (icc-ims-irispro-every-speed-spec-sheet.pdf, not spec1.pdf), populate the PDF title metadata, and ensure the text layer is real text rather than an image of a page.
Think carefully before gating. The site map proposes gating spec sheet downloads behind a contact form for lead generation. That is a defensible choice for sample reports and pillar guides. For spec sheets it is worth reconsidering: an engineer assembling a procurement specification is often working under a deadline and comparing several manufacturers, and a form is a reason to use the competitor’s ungated sheet instead. A gated spec sheet also cannot be indexed or cited, which forfeits the GEO value entirely. The recommended compromise is ungated individual spec sheets and a gated complete Equipment Catalog.
Keep one source of truth. The specification values in the PDFs and on the pages must match exactly. Maintain the values in one place and generate both from it.
